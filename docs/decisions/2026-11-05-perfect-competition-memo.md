# Perfect Competition — recommendation

**I recommend the farmer plant 10 tomato, 20 carrot, and 30 mesclun beds per season, giving him a profit of $42,762.**

## Why
Tomatoes become too expensive to continue planting. Although tomatoes earn the most revenue per bed ($8,800), their labor requirements increase quickly as more beds are planted. The 10th bed costs approximately $8,249 in additional costs, but the 11th costs approximately $9,391, exceeding its $8,800 revenue. Planting beyond 10 beds would reduce profit.

Carrots and mesclun remain profitable at their maximum planting limits. Their labor requirements increase more slowly, and each additional bed continues to generate more revenue than it costs to produce. Therefore, the farmer should plant all 20 carrot beds and all 30 mesclun beds.

Leaving four beds empty is intentional, not a mistake. The next tomato bed would cost more to produce than it earns, while carrots and mesclun have already reached their planting caps. Planting additional tomatoes would decrease overall profit.

## The judgment call
The model estimates that relaxing the carrot planting cap by one bed would increase profit by approximately $352, compared with approximately $246 for one additional mesclun bed. Therefore, the farmer should prioritize acquiring additional carrot-growing capacity, potentially through purchasing or leasing more land.

However, these estimates represent the value of only one additional bed under the current assumptions. The value of a second additional bed may differ because labor requirements continue to increase as more beds are planted. The model does not establish that additional beds would maintain the same profitability indefinitely.

## What would change my answer
A decrease in tomato prices would most significantly change the planting plan because tomatoes already reach their profitability limit at 10 beds, and lower prices could make even fewer tomato beds worthwhile.
